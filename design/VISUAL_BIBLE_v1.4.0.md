# Until Last Asteroid — v1.4.0 Visual Bible

## Status

- Visual direction approved from `design/concepts/gameplay_v1_4_mockup_v1.png`.
- Target: polished high-resolution 2D top-down sci-fi art, not pixel art and not
  perspective 3D.
- Logical gameplay space remains 1920 x 1080. Rendering must remain sharp on
  Full HD, 2K, and 4K displays.

## Readability rules

- Player and friendly projectiles use cyan and electric-blue emission.
- Enemies and hostile projectiles use restrained red and magenta emission.
- Asteroids remain neutral gray-brown and must silhouette clearly against space.
- Explosion cores may use white, amber, and orange.
- Background stars and nebulae must remain dimmer than interactive objects.
- Bloom follows luminous pixels and may never reveal a rectangular sprite or
  render-texture boundary.
- Effects must clarify an event before they decorate it: shot, hit, damage,
  destruction, and movement must remain visually distinct.
- HUD and crosshair remain sharp and are composed after gameplay bloom.

## Required production assets

### Level backgrounds

Each of the five current levels receives one unique 3840 x 2160 opaque far-space
background. Use high-quality JPG for final opaque images unless PNG produces a
material visual improvement worth its release size.

| Asset | Visual theme |
| --- | --- |
| `level_01_blue_nebula.jpg` | calm blue onboarding space |
| `level_02_violet_clouds.jpg` | blue-violet clouds |
| `level_03_asteroid_belt.jpg` | darker mineral region |
| `level_04_red_storm.jpg` | restrained hostile red nebula |
| `level_05_deep_void.jpg` | dark final prototype arena |

Background images contain the distant nebula and only subdued distant stars.
Mid-distance stars and near cosmic dust are generated by code so they can move
at different speeds and provide parallax without ten additional 4K textures.

### Ships

All ship sprites are transparent PNGs, point upward in their source image, use a
direct orthographic top-down view, and include no baked bloom, engine flame,
shadow, projectile, or background.

| Asset | Source canvas | Approx. gameplay footprint | Provisional collision radius |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| player ship | 256 x 256 | 110 x 135 | 38 |
| kamikaze saucer | 256 x 256 | 100 x 100 | 45 |
| shooter gunship | 256 x 256 | 139 x 67 | 42 (provisional) |

Transparent padding is allowed for rotation safety, but visual scale and
collision radius must be explicit data instead of being derived from the PNG
rectangle.

The circular kamikaze saucer continuously pursues the player while rotating at
a JSON-configured speed. The wide shooter gunship faces the player and fires
from alternating left and right weapon emitters every 1.0 second; both emitter
positions are normalized sprite coordinates stored in JSON.

The approved player ship is substantially longer than the previous sprite, so
its current circle is only provisional. Final collider shapes are selected after
the complete player, enemy, asteroid, and projectile art set is visible together.
For rotating ships, evaluate an oriented box, a capsule, and two or three
compound circles instead of using a non-rotating axis-aligned rectangle. Keep
asteroids circular unless their final silhouettes demonstrate a clear problem.

### Asteroids

- Four large variants on 256 x 256 canvases, collision radius about 46.
- Four small variants on 128 x 128 canvases, collision radius about 20.
- Direct top-down lighting with a consistent upper-left neutral key light.
- Tight silhouettes, transparent backgrounds, no baked explosion or glow.
- Small fragments visually inherit the same mineral family as the large
  asteroid from which they split. Large asteroids split directly into two small
  asteroids; small asteroids do not split again.

### Projectiles

| Asset | Source canvas | Notes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| player projectile | 64 x 128 | cyan-white energy core, points upward |
| enemy projectile | 64 x 128 | red-magenta energy core, points upward |

Only the compact projectile core belongs in the sprite. Muzzle flash, impact
sparks, and bloom are generated at runtime. Projectiles use a glow attached to
their current position and deliberately leave no trail or smoke behind them.

### Shared effect textures

- `soft_glow.png`, 128 x 128: neutral white radial glow mask.
- `spark.png`, 64 x 16: neutral white tapered streak.
- `smoke_puff.png`, 128 x 128: soft neutral cloud without baked colour.
- Six small asteroid/metal debris silhouettes, 64 x 64.
- `shockwave_ring.png`, 256 x 256: neutral ring mask.

Neutral masks are tinted by code, allowing one texture to serve cyan engines,
red hostile effects, orange explosions, and white impact flashes.

## Rendering architecture

### `GameplayBackground`

- Selects the far background from the current level's JSON data.
- Preserves aspect ratio using a cover crop; never stretches the image.
- Owns deterministic mid-star and near-dust layers.
- Moves layers slowly at different speeds without affecting game coordinates.

### `ParticleSystem`

- Owns a reusable contiguous collection of particles.
- Supports emission rate, burst count, lifetime, position, velocity, rotation,
  angular velocity, size, colour, alpha curve, and drag.
- Draws particles in batches grouped by texture and render layer.
- Supports `BehindEntities`, `World`, `AboveEntities`, and `Emissive` layers.
- Starts without a fixed object pool; allocation behaviour is profiled under a
  chaotic test scene before pooling is considered.

### `GameplayEffects`

- Translates gameplay events into visual effects.
- Exposes requests such as player/enemy muzzle flash, asteroid/ship hit,
  asteroid/ship explosion, engine emission, and camera shake.
- Contains effect recipes, not combat rules or damage calculations.
- Keeps visual timing synchronized with the existing sound event.

### `GameplayRenderer`

Composes the frame in this order:

1. far background;
2. procedural star and dust parallax;
3. particles behind entities;
4. gameplay entities;
5. trails, impacts, debris, and foreground particles;
6. controlled emissive bloom;
7. stable HUD;
8. gameplay crosshair and state overlays.

The normal scene renders at the physical viewport resolution, not into a fixed
1920 x 1080 texture that is later stretched. Bloom may use a deliberately
downsampled emissive-only buffer after profiling because it contains blurred
light, never sharp scene imagery.

### Camera shake

- Applies a short offset to the gameplay view only.
- Never moves the HUD or crosshair.
- Uses low amplitude for player damage and higher amplitude for large
  explosions.
- Must be bounded and composable when events overlap.

## Data-driven additions

Extend level data with a background identifier. Add visual configuration for:

- sprite path and visual scale;
- explicit collision radius;
- engine emitter positions;
- particle recipe parameters;
- projectile and explosion colours;
- bloom intensity;
- camera-shake duration and amplitude.

Collider geometry is gameplay balance and remains explicit even if the artwork
is replaced later. The JSON schema may evolve from one radius to a shape plus
shape-specific parameters after the final sprite review.

## Initial effect set

- Continuous player engine exhaust while thrust input is active.
- Continuous enemy engine exhaust where the ship design exposes an engine.
- Player and enemy muzzle flashes.
- Persistent cyan/red projectile glow without trails or smoke.
- Stone sparks/dust for asteroid hits.
- Energy/metal sparks for ship hits.
- Large and small asteroid explosions with scaled debris counts.
- Player and enemy ship explosions with a flash, sparks, fragments, smoke, and
  shockwave.
- Small damage shake and large-explosion shake.

## Performance budget and validation

- Preserve uncapped menu/gameplay performance when VSync and the limiter are off.
- Avoid one draw call per particle where batching is practical.
- Avoid loading textures or shaders during gameplay.
- Avoid per-frame creation of render textures.
- Stress-test at least 100 active entities and a burst of 1,000 particles.
- Verify Fullscreen, Windowed, and Borderless at Full HD and the highest locally
  available display resolution.
- Verify pause capture/blur, focus-loss pause, screen wrapping, and effects near
  every world edge.

The final Release benchmark on the development machine measured approximately
0.89 ms per frame for 100 simulated entities (4,950 possible collision pairs)
and 0.62 ms per frame for 1,000 particles across seven render batches. The
combined 1.51 ms result does not justify object pooling for v1.4.0. The benchmark
source is retained at `tests/PerformanceBenchmark.cpp` for future comparisons.

## Explicitly outside v1.4.0

- New weapons, bonuses, enemies, bosses, campaign, saves, and level selection.
- Slow motion.
- Mandatory object pooling without profiling evidence.
- Dedicated non-lethal hit sounds until suitable audio assets are selected.
